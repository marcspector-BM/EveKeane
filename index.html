About the Author

Evelyn Goldfinch

Evelyn Goldfinch is a musician who carves out a living in a small town in the hills of Pennsylvania, where she resides with her two worshipped and completely adorable cats, Moses and Mirabel.

Her writing draws from a life rich with experience — the quiet beauty of rural life, the complexity of human connection, and the search for meaning in an often chaotic world. With raw honesty and dark humor, Evelyn invites readers into her world, offering stories that resonate long after the final page.

About the Book

A Star So Far from Heaven

The heart is a solitary hunter, searching for love, connection, and meaning. A Star So Far from Heaven is an intimate memoir that chronicles a deeply personal journey through love, loss, and the pursuit of self-worth. Evelyn Goldfinch masterfully weaves together moments of joy, heartbreak, and resilience, inviting readers into her world with raw honesty and dark humor.

This story challenges the idea that we are merely a product of chance, reflecting instead on the profound intricacies of the human experience. Through reflections on people, places, and relationships, A Star So Far from Heaven speaks to anyone who has ever felt lost in the vastness of life, seeking a place to belong.

Goldfinch's poignant narrative is not just about love — it's about the resilience of the human spirit, the courage to embrace one's true self, and the realization that even the farthest star can shine brilliantly in the darkest sky.

Whether you are navigating your own path of self-discovery or simply seeking an evocative and thought-provoking read, this book is sure to leave a lasting impression.

From the Book

“

The heart is a solitary hunter, searching for love, connection, and meaning. I have spent most of my life looking outward, expecting someone else to complete the picture, to fill the empty spaces with their presence, their approval, their love.

But the truth I have come to understand is this: no one can complete you. You must arrive whole, or not at all. The search for self-worth is not a destination — it is a homecoming. A return to the self you left behind somewhere along the way, buried beneath the weight of expectation, fear, and the quiet desperation of trying to be enough.

This is not a story about finding love. It is a story about finding yourself, so that when love does arrive — real love, the kind that asks nothing of you but your truth — you will be ready to receive it.

”
